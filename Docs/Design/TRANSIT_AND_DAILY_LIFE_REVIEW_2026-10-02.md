## Assessment

Increasing elevator patience alone will not solve this. The model combines minutes-per-floor elevator travel, compulsory long stair alternatives, concentrated destinations and repetitive routines. Ordinary operation should let Residents spend their day working, sleeping, eating and socializing; congestion should emerge from actual capacity, layout and incidents. Longer visible queues can be credible without making multi-hour waits normal.

The [complete-demand checkpoint](../Validation/C0_CHECKPOINT_2026-10-02.md) measured tier1 757/1966 required episodes completed within360 ticks (38.5%), p95 terminal queue wait340, live maximum4171; tier5 950/2094 (45.4%), p95248, maximum1273. Both FAIL. These are historical executed measurements, not new runs during this review. Keep >=99% completion within360, p95<=60 including zero-wait demand, live maximum<=240 and no unresolved required unreachable demand. Add elevator-user diagnostics because local successes can dilute aggregate queue percentiles.

## Proposed implementation order

1. **Repair correctness and instrument a day.** Correct live wait/FIFO accounting and explicit arbitration results. Preserve the observer before planning. Record demand purpose, origin/destination, mode, stairs floors, arrival lateness and daily time spent waiting/riding/walking/at activities. No missing demand may disappear through a retry or new schedule identity.
2. **Calibrate movement time.** Keep the fixed deterministic simulation tick. Use accumulated sub-minute movement progress or bounded domain substeps so several floors can be traversed per minute without making each floor a several-minute event. Calibrate hallway, ascent/descent, doors and boarding together. The exact speed table needs measured design experiments; no new values are certified here. Preserve progress/remainders in saves.
3. **Make mode choice credible.** Use physical arrival ETA plus bounded preference/effort cost, with deterministic tie-breaking. Short flights should often favor stairs; long ordinary journeys should favor a usable elevator. Compute stairs from directional traversal plus hallway time and cumulative exertion; compute elevators from pickup/seat ETA, ride/stops and hallway time. Reconsider only at bounded cadence with hysteresis, retaining queue position until a feasible alternative is accepted. A starting experiment is1–2 floors normally eligible,3–4 downward floors conditional on advantage,10–15 live ticks before reconsideration; these are provisional game design values, not real-world measurements. Mobility constraints apply where modeled; do not silently expand M11 health scope. Long stairs remain exceptional escape when elevators are unavailable, and missed deadlines remain failures.
4. **Diversify obligations and destinations.** Assign early/day/late/overnight/flexible cohorts from actual jobs, rather than uniform random staggering. Traits adjust those obligations. Add preparation, breakfast, lunch, home time and optional outings; bedtime follows the shift. Distinguish appointments from flexible windows. Persist individual offsets and deterministic day choices; support overnight blocks and episode identity across midnight. Departures should anticipate appointment time; lateness remains visible rather than extending every workday from arrival. Resolve amenities using current location, reachability, opening/staffing/capacity and stable preferences. Home meals need a real resource/provider rule, never free need replenishment. Apply measured daily fatigue budgets, distinguish queue/riding from stairs, and initialize needs consistently with routine phase.
5. **Improve dispatch and balance tiers.** Directional hall demand, compatible boarding, stable pickup commitments, bounded oldest-call protection and actual stop/seat ETA. Improve service at each tier, then expose investment/shaft space/upkeep/energy/resilience tradeoffs. Tier1 should work for a modest or distributed Tower; a tall dense Tower should need legal upgrades/layout. Never add a fourth car beyond the existing cap or claim upgrading restores lost reference seats: the reference already has3×20 seats and upgrades preserve them.

Illustrative capacity check, **not measured throughput**: three20-seat cars shuttling F25↔F0 with two6-tick stop cycles deliver60 seats per `(2×25×travelTicks +12)` ticks: about.23 seats/tick at tier1 and.37 at tier5. A200-person batch takes roughly873/540 ticks in that simplified repeat-shuttle model. Actual mixed-origin service differs; the example shows why speed, stops and demand structure must be evaluated together.

Do not stagger demand in response to observed queues merely to pass acceptance, restore needs for free, teleport, relax deadlines or suppress failures. Legitimate peaks should remain. Distributed staffed amenities and sensible work shifts are systems the Steward can shape, not individual Resident orders.
