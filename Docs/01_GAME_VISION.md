# Game Vision

## One sentence

Build one enormous mixed-use tower, then live with the society that forms inside it.

## Format

- Genre: vertical-city builder and emergent society simulation.
- Presentation: controlled 2.5D architectural cutaway with modular 2D characters.
- Audience: players of city builders and colony simulations who enjoy legible cause and effect.
- Initial release: premium single-player desktop game.
- Tone: optimistic but fragile. A thriving tower feels vibrant; neglect and weak systems produce visible personal hardship without becoming pure misery or cartoon villainy.

## Player role

The player is the tower's Steward: developer, mayor, and systems designer. They shape structure, services, leases, policy, capacity, and zone-level priorities; inspect outcomes; and respond to crises. They cannot directly order an individual resident to perform an action. Control is exercised through systems, not personal commands.

## Core loop

1. Build space and infrastructure.
2. Populate it through housing and business demand.
3. Observe daily life and resident state.
4. Diagnose spatial, social, and systemic problems through overlays and inspectors.
5. Respond through building, capacity, policy, leasing, or zone-level priorities.
6. Watch the population adapt.

## Product pillars

1. **Height is geography.** Elevators are streets and transfer lobbies are intersections.
2. **Architecture produces society.** Contact, access, cost, and shared grievances shape relationships and factions.
3. **Every major problem is explainable.** The player can move from symptom to overlay to inspector cause to system intervention.
4. **Scale without micromanagement.** Blueprints, zoning, leasing, soft priorities, and aggregated simulation let one tower grow into a city.
5. **People remain personal.** Inspectable lives, needs, and local relationship networks make aggregate systems emotionally legible.
6. **Systems create pressure and relief.** Internal Strain and external Scrutiny rise and fall with player decisions.

## Resident wellbeing and social pressure

Residents are autonomous agents governed by three readable time horizons:

- **Needs → Focus** (short-term): Hunger, Energy, Social, Hygiene, and Purpose affect immediate choices and work quality.
- **Satisfaction → Grievances** (medium-term): Commute quality, crowding, noise, rent burden, service access, and recent events accumulate into wellbeing and complaints.
- **Strain** (long-term): personality-filtered cumulative pressure that can lead to move-outs, mental-strain events, or faction radicalisation.

A small, high-impact personality facet set makes reactions meaningfully different without an opaque, large trait matrix. Relationships and shared grievances shape faction affinity. The player creates the conditions in which residents thrive or struggle; they never direct an individual response.

## External pressure: Scrutiny

**Scrutiny** represents attention and pressure from the wider city. It rises with rapid expansion, inequality, unresolved crises, and aggressive policy; it falls through balanced policy, service investment, and successful responses. High Scrutiny can increase external-event pressure and temporarily constrain expansion. It remains abstract and overlay-readable, never a cartoon-invasion loop.

## First playable

Five floors, fifty residents, apartments, offices, one shop, utilities, stairs, and elevators. Home/work/food trips create congestion. The elevator overlay explains the problem. Added capacity improves commute time and satisfaction. The proof demonstrates the complete chain: world symptom → overlay → inspector cause → systems-level response → measurable improvement.

## Beta boundary

- 30 floors and 300 persistent residents.
- Residential, office, retail, restaurant, clinic, maintenance, security, utility, and lobby rooms.
- Passenger transit, power, water, waste, and maintenance with degradation and technician response.
- Households, workplaces, friendships, grievances, and four faction archetypes: Tenant Union, Corporate, Merchants, and Eco Council.
- Soft specialist roles emerging from capacity and training systems.
- The Needs → Satisfaction → Strain model and Scrutiny pressure resource.
- Eight overlays and adaptive, systems-driven crisis pressure with personal consequences.
- One campaign goal: sustain City Status.

Deferred: multiplayer, multiple towers, street simulation, detailed crime/politics, full furniture editing, mobile/console, user-facing generation, and large personality matrices.
