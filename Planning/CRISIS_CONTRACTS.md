## 1. Experience and design decisions

A crisis makes an existing system failure legible, turns sustained pressure into a bounded incident, and lets the Steward observe whether a systems response actually helped. The six experiences are different: tenant liquidity leverage; organized lost productive labor; dependent service failure; travel demand exceeding viable routing; unsafe flowing water; and an external scoped inspection. Merely sharing a phase enum does not make them adaptive.

Adaptation means eligibility follows live causes; scope follows documented social/physical dependencies; effective interventions change measured phase progression; ineffective interventions do not; and residual consequences persist. We choose existing owners and narrowly added adapters, rather than another simulation framework. Residents remain autonomous. Caretaker remains the delivered explicit exception and never substitutes for a named chain.

### Agency and pacing

Civil entry deliberately uses existing social organization: same issue, membership and meaningful contact history; isolated distress is not instant collective action. Infrastructure entry distinguishes transient overload from sustained disruption. Crackdown requires both persistent external pressure and actual exposure-producing operations. Water has a true contaminant source, not a renamed pressure shortage.

Observed prototype utility wear is .006 **per tick**, not per day (about53 ticks from warning to mechanical failure). The selected foundation model replaces it with .006/hour: full-condition warning threshold after3000 ticks and Warning after15 qualifying samples at3014, failure7000 ticks if unattended, then120 actual outage ticks for Active. Old short traces are historical evidence only. UI must expose elapsed/remaining ticks, pause and speed controls; no wall-clock response-time promise is made without measuring actual speed. Native pacing/playtest acceptance belongs to implementation; this design flags its tuning risk.
